# Motion Directions

Choose one dominant direction per segment. A 3-10 second fragment should not mix many motion languages.

## 1. Restrained documentary motion

Use for history, institutions, biography, archive, policy, crises, and reflective segments.

Traits:

- slow fade and slight drift
- subtle parallax between paper layers
- map/document/photo-like plates move gently
- restrained line reveal or stamp reveal
- calm hold time

Best for:

- documentary inserts
- chapter cards
- archival backgrounds
- quote or concept cards

Avoid:

- fast zooms
- bounce
- fake conspiracy-board motion
- flashy transitions

## 2. Clean finance explainer motion

Use for explaining concepts, mechanisms, incentives, and cause-effect relationships.

Traits:

- initial visibility and reveals follow the explanation; title-first is useful when the title establishes the idea, not mandatory
- arrows, lines, or blocks reveal in sequence
- highlight one keyword or number at a time
- motion follows reading order
- clear hold after each reveal

Best for:

- key concept segments
- simple diagrams
- finance mechanism explainers
- PNG element motion

Avoid:

- animating all labels together
- tiny fast-moving text
- overdesigned UI dashboard feel

## 3. Data-news pacing

Use when numbers, rankings, timelines, or comparisons drive the segment.

Traits:

- chart elements may build from baseline when a build is meaningful; an existing comparison can stay fully visible
- numbers count or step into place only when meaningful, with matching visible geometry
- labels may already be visible, reveal with geometry, or follow it according to the explanation
- pacing is tighter than documentary motion
- transitions are clean and factual

Best for:

- bar chart reveals
- timeline evolution
- market comparison
- risk scale

Avoid:

- 3D charts
- decorative chart junk
- fake data
- unreadable micro labels

## 4. Handmade annotation motion

Use when the video should feel like reasoning on paper or explaining a concept by hand.

Traits:

- hand-drawn underline
- circle or arrow sketch-in
- paper label slides in
- marker highlight sweep
- small jitter may exist but must be controlled

Best for:

- keyword emphasis
- “look here” moments
- finance concept simplification
- documentary evidence callouts

Avoid:

- childish doodle overload
- excessive scribble noise
- comedic sticker effects

## 5. Editorial title-card motion

Use for clean segment openers and section breaks.

Traits:

- typography-led hierarchy
- mask reveal, vertical/horizontal slide, or subtle scale
- one visual symbol or texture layer
- enough hold for reading
- transition-ready end frame

Best for:

- title card
- section separator
- cover-like frame
- finance/documentary chapter marker

Avoid:

- generic intro templates
- big elastic bounce
- spinning text

## 6. Symbolic PNG element motion

Use when transparent PNG cutouts support narration.

Traits:

- one object enters with purposeful direction
- object can point, stack, reveal, stamp, slide, or pin
- scale changes are subtle
- shadow and depth are consistent with background

Best for:

- paper labels
- arrows
- stamps
- chart blocks
- map pins
- icons
- cutaway props

Avoid:

- random floating stickers
- too many PNG elements competing
- inconsistent shadows

## 7. Ambient background loop

Use for looping plates behind text, charts, or narration.

Traits:

- very slow motion
- low contrast movement
- no important detail at edges
- calm zone for overlays
- seamless or near-seamless ending

Best for:

- background plates
- documentary atmosphere
- title card beds
- chart overlay backgrounds

Avoid:

- motion that distracts from foreground text
- high-frequency particles
- blue-purple neon glow
